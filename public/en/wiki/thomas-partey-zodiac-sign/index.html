Sun Sign vs Rising Sign: What's Confirmed and What Isn't

The Thomas Partey zodiac sign debate really turns on the gap between two placements people treat as equal: the Sun sign and the rising sign. They work differently. The Sun sign comes from the date alone, so his Gemini is fixed and checkable. The rising sign, or Ascendant, depends on the exact minute and location of birth, because it is the zodiac degree climbing over the horizon at that instant — and it shifts roughly every two minutes. Without a confirmed time, his Ascendant is a guess, and so is every house placement built on top of it. Put plainly: change the assumed minute of birth and the rising sign can slide to a neighbouring sign, while the Gemini Sun does not move at all.

There is a clear trade-off in how profile sites handle this. To get a tidy, complete-looking chart, those sites sacrifice accuracy: they default the birth time to noon and publish the houses as if they were real. To get an honest read instead, you sacrifice completeness — you keep the confirmed Sun and the sign's Mercury rulership, and you set the houses aside as theoretical. As technique writers like Robert Hand have long stressed, the house framework only means something once the birth time is locked. So the choice is precision over tidiness, or tidiness over precision; you cannot have both from an unconfirmed birth time. If his exact time is ever published, the houses can be recomputed and verified; until then, the careful read simply leaves them open.
